# The Compounding Firm: A One-Page Brief

By Craig Horton, Craig Horton Advisory.

## The problem

Every company is buying AI. Most are buying the same models. So the model is not
where the advantage comes from, and very soon it will not be where any advantage
comes from. The risk is plain: if all the value flows to a handful of model makers,
firms become tenants of their own expertise, and whole industries get hollowed out
the way outsourcing hollowed out manufacturing.

## The idea

A firm's lasting advantage is not the model it picks. It is the learning loop it
owns. Two assets compound inside that loop. Human capital is the knowledge,
judgment, and relationships of your people. Token capital is the AI capability you
build and own. They grow together: your people set the goals and supply the
judgment, and the system turns that judgment into something repeatable and
scalable. You can hand off a task, but you can never hand off your learning.

## The test that matters

There is one test for whether you own your AI capability rather than rent it. You
should be able to swap out a general-purpose model and keep your company-veteran
expertise. If swapping the model wipes your expertise, you were renting it. If the
expertise stays, you own it.

## What we built

We made that ownable thing concrete. It is a single portable file we call Veteran
Capital. It holds your firm's notes, its private way of judging good work, and the
learned corrections that steer any model toward your standard. Hand that file to a
different model and your veteran comes with it. Your AI capability becomes a file
you own, version, and audit.

Alongside it we built a working learning loop: software that does your firm's work,
checks each answer against your own gold standard, learns the gap, and folds the
learning back into the file so it improves with use.

## What we proved

Two things, both demonstrated in running code, not slides.

The expertise transfers. A file built on one model measurably improved a different
model it had never seen. The veteran lived in the file, not the model.

The advantage lasts. As the underlying models got stronger, the firm's edge did not
disappear. Better models rode on top of the owned layer instead of erasing it. That
is a moat that survives commoditisation.

## Why it matters to you

It turns a thesis into something you can show a board: here is the asset you would
own, and here is the proof it is yours. It protects the value your people create, it
keeps that value inside your firm and your community, and it does not depend on
betting right on any single model. The priority is a frontier ecosystem, not just a
frontier model, where every organisation can compound its own human and token
capital. This is how a firm creates value for itself and for the economy around it.

## The honest caveat

This is a proof of the mechanism on sample data, not a return-on-investment figure.
The next step is to point it at your real workflows and a live model.
