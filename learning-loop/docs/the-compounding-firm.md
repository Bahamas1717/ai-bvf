# The Compounding Firm

Human Capital, Token Capital, And The Learning Loop That Owns Your Future

By Craig Horton, Craig Horton Advisory. The Transformation Brief.

## The Shift Nobody Is Pricing In

For thirty years we used digital systems to enhance human capital. This is the
first time we can build a real cognitive loop between people and digital systems,
and that changes how we conceive of work inside an enterprise. The question is no
longer which tool to deploy. It is whether your organisation keeps learning, builds
IP, and differentiates in a world where models can absorb the expertise of people
and firms and commoditise it.

## Two Forms Of Capital

Every company now builds two assets that compound.

Human capital is the knowledge, judgment, relationships, ingenuity, and pattern
recognition of its people. Token capital is the AI capability the firm builds and
owns. The mistake is to treat these as substitutes, where token capital grows as
human capital shrinks. They are complements. Human capital becomes more valuable as
token capital grows, because human agency is what drives token capital forward.
People set ambitious goals, connect dots across domains, build the relationships,
and recognise the patterns that matter. Without human direction you have compute
running in circles.

## The Real Asset Is The Loop, Not The Model

The durable asset is not the model you pick. It is the learning loop you build on
top of swappable models, where human and token capital compound. You can offload a
task, or even a job, but you can never offload your learning. I think of the loop as
a hill-climbing machine. Unlike most assets it compounds: every improved workflow
generates better training signal, which accelerates the accumulation of tacit
knowledge unique to the firm. The companies that build this early hold an advantage
that is hard to replicate, regardless of the next model release.

## The Veteran Test

There is a single test for whether a firm actually owns its AI capability. You must
be able to swap out a generalist base model without losing the company veteran
expertise encoded in your system. If swapping the model resets your expertise, the
value lived in the model, and you were renting it. If the expertise stays, it lived
in your firm, and you own it.

Making that test concrete requires the expertise to be a thing you can hold: a
portable, model-agnostic artifact. That is what we call Veteran Capital. It bundles
the firm's institutional memory, its private evals, and a learned calibration layer
that corrects any base model toward the firm's own judgment. Hand that file to a
different model and the veteran comes with it. Your AI capability becomes a file you
own, version, and audit.

## What The Loop Is Made Of

Four parts, and each is firm-owned.

Agentic workflows that improve over time while the firm retains control of its IP.
Private evals that measure whether a model is improving against business outcomes
that matter to the firm, not external benchmarks. Private reinforcement and feedback
environments that let models grow stronger on real traces from inside the
organisation. A knowledge base that makes institutional memory queryable and makes
token use more efficient. Human agency injects direction at every stage. The loop is
where that direction compounds.

## The Stakes

The last thing any of us should want is a world where every company in every sector
cedes value to a handful of models that eat everything they see. If all the value
accrues to a few models, the political economy will not tolerate it, and there is no
societal permission for an AI future that hollows out entire industries. We have seen
this film. The first wave of globalisation hollowed out industrial economies. The GDP
numbers looked fine on the surface, and the displacement was real, and the
consequences are still being felt.

So the priority is a frontier ecosystem, not just a frontier model, where value flows
broadly across every company, every industry, and every country. Every organisation
should be able to own the learning loop that encodes its institutional knowledge, and
compound its human and token capital. When that happens, companies create value for
themselves and for the economy around them. Employees see their expertise amplified
and their judgment become part of systems that make it replicable and scalable, and
the benefits accrue to the companies and communities around them. That is the stable
equilibrium we should build together.
